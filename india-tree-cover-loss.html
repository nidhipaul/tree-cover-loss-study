India has lost 7.4% of its tree cover this century

2.43 million hectares of canopy came down between 2001 and 2025, but the loss is not spread evenly. Eight northeastern states hold 35% of India's forest and account for 74% of its loss, and most of that is shifting cultivation on a rotational cycle rather than permanent clearing. What does Canopy cover >30% mean?

How thick did the tree cover have to be in 2000 for a patch of land to count as forest at all? Every 30 m square of India has a score for how much of it sat under canopy that year — 5%, 40%, 90%. The threshold is where you draw the line. At >30%, only squares that were at least 30% covered are counted; the rest are ignored.

It defines forest. It says nothing about how much was later lost. Set the line low and patchy scrub counts too, so the totals grow; set it high and only dense forest counts.

>30% is the standard that gets published.

India's own Forest Survey uses >10%, so that chip is the closest match to Indian official figures. Drivers and primary forest are only measured at >30%.

Before you quote these figures

Four things this dataset does not say, each of which is routinely misread.

01 Loss is not deforestation

"Tree cover" is any vegetation over 5 m — natural forest and plantation alike — and "loss" is canopy removal from any cause: harvesting, fire, disease, storm damage. Much of it regrows. Permanence is not in this measurement.

02 Shifting cultivation is a cycle, not a clearance

61% of driver-attributed loss is shifting cultivation — largely jhum in the northeast, where land is cleared, cropped and left to regrow. It registers as loss every rotation. Reading it as forest destroyed double-counts the same hillsides.

03 The early years are not comparable to the late ones

Satellite inputs and methods changed between 2011 and 2015, improving detection. Part of the apparent rise from a 54,000 ha/yr average in 2001–05 to 124,000 ha/yr in 2021–25 is the instrument getting better, not only the forest getting worse.

04 Primary forest here means humid tropical only

The primary-forest layer covers 23 states with humid tropical forest — the Western Ghats, the northeast, the islands. Himalayan temperate and dry deciduous forests are outside its scope, so a blank is not an absence of old-growth.

How this was compiled. Tree cover and its loss come from the University of Maryland's GLAD laboratory working with Google, derived from Landsat imagery at 30 m; a pixel is recorded as lost where a stand-replacing disturbance clears at least half its canopy. Humid tropical primary forest follows Turubanova et al.'s definition — mature natural forest never completely cleared and regrown in recent history. Dominant drivers were mapped by WRI with Google DeepMind, which assigns each 1 km cell the single driver responsible for the majority of its loss across the whole period. Carbon emissions, removals and net flux are modelled from Harris et al., revised by Gibbs et al., covering CO2, CH4 and N2O across multiple carbon pools, with a negative net flux indicating a sink. Administrative boundaries are GADM 4.1.
