Kenya has lost 12.4% of its tree cover this century

410,801 hectares of canopy came down between 2001 and 2025, from a forest covering less than 6% of the country. But the trend has turned: loss peaked in 2007, and the last five years have run at under half the 2006–10 rate. Narok county alone accounts for one hectare in five, and nearly three-quarters of all attributed loss is conversion to permanent cropland. Read the caveats before quoting any number here.

What does Canopy cover >30% mean?

How thick did the tree cover have to be in 2000 for a patch of land to count as forest at all? Every 30 m square of Kenya has a score for how much of it sat under canopy that year — 5%, 40%, 90%. The threshold is where you draw the line. At >30%, only squares that were at least 30% covered are counted; the rest are ignored.

It defines forest. It says nothing about how much was later lost. Set the line low and patchy scrub counts too, so the totals grow; set it high and only dense forest counts.

>30% is the standard that gets published.

Kenya's Constitution targets 10% tree cover of total land area — that is a share of the country, not a canopy density, so it is not this setting. Drivers and primary forest are only measured at >30%.

Before you quote these figures

Four things this dataset does not say, each of which is routinely misread.

01 Loss is not deforestation

"Tree cover" is any vegetation over 5 m — natural forest and plantation alike — and "loss" is canopy removal from any cause: harvesting, fire, disease, storm damage. Much of it regrows. Permanence is not in this measurement.

02 This loss is unusually likely to be permanent

72% of driver-attributed loss is conversion to permanent agriculture and a further 24% is logging. Unlike rotational systems that clear and regrow, cropland conversion rarely returns to forest — so here, more of the loss than usual really does mean forest gone for good.

03 The early years are not comparable to the late ones

Satellite inputs and methods changed between 2011 and 2015, improving detection. That normally inflates later years — which makes Kenya's fall from 21,300 ha/yr in 2006–10 to 10,000 ha/yr in 2021–25 more convincing, not less: the decline shows up despite the instrument getting better at finding loss.

04 Primary forest here means humid tropical only

The primary-forest layer reaches 35 of Kenya's 47 counties and accounts for just 55,025 ha of loss. Dryland and montane forest outside the humid tropical zone falls outside its scope, so a blank county is not an absence of old-growth.

How this was compiled. Tree cover and its loss come from the University of Maryland's GLAD laboratory working with Google, derived from Landsat imagery at 30 m; a pixel is recorded as lost where a stand-replacing disturbance clears at least half its canopy. Humid tropical primary forest follows Turubanova et al.'s definition — mature natural forest never completely cleared and regrown in recent history. Dominant drivers were mapped by WRI with Google DeepMind, which assigns each 1 km cell the single driver responsible for the majority of its loss across the whole period. Carbon emissions, removals and net flux are modelled from Harris et al., revised by Gibbs et al., covering CO2, CH4 and N2O across multiple carbon pools, with a negative net flux indicating a sink. Administrative boundaries are GADM 4.1.
