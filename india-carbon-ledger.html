India's forest carbon ledger closes 78.8M tonnes CO₂e in the black every year

At >30% canopy cover, India's 634 districts remove an estimated 133.4 million tonnes of CO₂e a year and emit 54.6 million — a net sink of 78.8 million tonnes. That national balance nets out two very different ledgers: shifting-cultivation districts in the northeast running deep in the red, offset by mangrove and Western Ghats districts banking enormous surpluses.

How to read a carbon ledger

Carbon stock is the aboveground biomass carbon standing in a district's forest in 2000, in megagrams of carbon (Mg C).

Gross emissions are CO₂-equivalent released when that biomass is cleared, burned or decays, averaged per year.

Gross removals are CO₂e drawn down by forest growth over the same period.

Net flux is removals minus emissions: negative is a net sink (more carbon absorbed than released), positive is a net source.

A district can show heavy tree cover loss and still be a net sink, if regrowth elsewhere in its forest outpaces what was cleared — and the reverse also holds.

Carbon flux — Harris, N.L., D.A. Gibbs, A. Baccini, et al. 2021, Nature Climate Change 11: 234–240; revised in Gibbs, D.A., M. Rose, et al. 2025, ESSD 17(3): 1217–1243.
